# Photography styles: Moriyama, Winogrand, Cartier-Bresson, Meyerowitz

Research date: 2026-10-29.

Scope: visual style, composition, light, tonal range, colour, texture, timing, and emotional effect. The user added Joel Meyerowitz during research. Interpret “winograd” as Garry Winogrand.

## Verified findings and sources

### Daido Moriyama

- [LACMA: Fracture](https://www.lacma.org/art/exhibition/fracture-daido-moriyama) identifies high contrast, grain, tilted viewpoints, and are, bure, boke: grainy, blurry, out of focus. The exhibition includes colour photographs and books.
- [BOMB interview with Moriyama](https://bombmagazine.org/articles/2017/01/04/daido-moriyama/) records his preference for strong black and contrast. He describes advertisements and screens as immediate parts of everyday experience. He links photographs with memory and viewers' interpretations. He also discusses discarded negatives and reuse.
- [Aperture interview](https://aperture.org/editorial/daido-moriyama-on-the-unending-newness-of-photographs/) confirms his continued preference for black and white. Digital capture lets him shoot in colour and convert images later. Reproduction in books, magazines, and exhibitions can change how an image appears.
- [Finnish Museum of Photography retrospective](https://www.valokuvataiteenmuseo.fi/en/exhibitions/daido-moriyama-retrospective) places the work in postwar consumer society and describes a more lyrical development after his crisis in the early 1980s. It confirms colour and digital work.
- [Fondation Cartier collection](https://www.fondationcartier.com/en/collection/artists/daido-moriyama) discusses eroticism, movement, grain, and his relationship with Provoke. Major books include Japan: A Photo Theater (1968) and Farewell Photography (1972).

Interpretation: tactile, restless, intimate, uneasy, sensual, and sometimes dreamlike. Dark areas and lost detail can make an ordinary scene feel unstable. This describes tendencies, not every photograph.

### Garry Winogrand

- [MoMA: John Szarkowski, The Work of Garry Winogrand](https://www.moma.org/momaorg/shared/pdfs/docs/learn/courses/Szarkowski_1988_Work_of_Garry_Winogrand.pdf) explains the tilted frame as a compositional response to wide-angle perspective. It should not be reduced to a random stylistic trick.
- [Denver Art Museum: tilted frame and repetition](https://www.denverartmuseum.org/en/edu/lesson/more-things-change-more-they-stay-same) explains the varied purposes of tilt, including inclusion, emphasis, and balance.
- [Brooklyn Museum: Garry Winogrand: Color](https://opencollection.brooklynmuseum.org/exhibitions/3373) documents Kodachrome, slower early colour film, later faster stocks, and the use of colour as another element of a picture. Search results expose full exhibition text, but a direct open returned a 404 page. Use this source with that access limitation recorded.
- [SFMOMA retrospective release](https://www.sfmoma.org/press/release/first-major-touring-exhibition-of-garry-winogrand/) describes crowded frames, events at the edges, humour, isolation, prosperity, and unease. It also discusses the unresolved archive and selection after his death.
- [SFMOMA catalogue](https://www.sfmoma.org/publication/garry-winogrand/) supports the wide geographic and emotional scope of his American work.
- [MOCA: World's Fair, N.Y., 1964](https://www.moca.org/artworks/worlds-fair-ny) provides an example for studying several simultaneous gestures.

Interpretation: energetic, comic, socially awkward, crowded, and unresolved. His clear description of several events differs from Moriyama's frequent loss of detail.

### Henri Cartier-Bresson

- [Foundation: Cartier-Bresson's statement](https://www.henricartierbresson.org/en/hcb/) connects intuition, spontaneity, geometry, and the simultaneous recognition of an event and the organization of forms.
- [University of Michigan Museum of Art](https://umma.umich.edu/exhibitions/in-focus-henri-cartier-bresson/) discusses Renaissance geometry, Surrealism, ordinary human activity, and movement.
- [Foundation: landscape exhibition press kit](https://www.henricartierbresson.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/DP_HCB_Lexpe%CC%81rience-du-paysage_EN-2.pdf) describes proportion, planes, balance, and contemplative landscape work.
- [Foundation: China exhibition](https://www.henricartierbresson.org/en/expositions/henri-cartier-bresson-chine-1948-1949-1958/) documents poetic, detached reportage attentive to people and composition.
- [LIFE: China in colour](https://time.com/3872061/cartier-bresson-red-china-in-color-1958/) confirms colour work in 1958. Avoid saying he never photographed in colour.
- [MoMA: The Early Work](https://www.moma.org/documents/moma_catalogue_2165_300296079.pdf) warns against reducing the whole career to the decisive moment.
- [Paris Musées: Hyères, France, 1932](https://www.parismuseescollections.paris.fr/fr/musee-d-art-moderne/oeuvres/hyeres-france) supplies an example of architecture and passing movement.

Interpretation: poised, lyrical, humane, alert, and sometimes strange. Compared with Moriyama, grey tones and spatial relationships usually remain more legible. Contrast varies by image and print.

### Joel Meyerowitz

- [Artist: selected works](https://www.joelmeyerowitz.com/) separates street colour, black and white, early colour, Cape Light, portraits, still life, and other bodies of work.
- [Artist: Cape Light](https://www.joelmeyerowitz.com/cape-light/) supplies visual references for his coastal palette and atmospheric light.
- [Howard Greenberg Gallery](https://www.howardgreenberg.com/artists/83-joel-meyerowitz/) documents early advocacy for colour, Cape Light (1978), Wild Flowers (1983), portraiture, and landscape.
- [Mount Holyoke exhibition guide](https://artmuseum.mtholyoke.edu/wp-content/uploads/pdfs/Fragile_Paper_Timeships_UA.pdf) confirms an 8-by-10-inch view camera and colour negative film for the exhibited later work. It describes the emotional role of light, detail, and spaces between objects.
- [Smith College Museum of Art](https://scma.smith.edu/blog/eye-street-joel-meyerowitz) describes his transition from advertising to street work and his commitment to colour.
- [Florida Museum of Photographic Arts](https://www.fmopa.org/joel-meyerowitz-photographs/) discusses both black-and-white and colour work, including New York streets and Cape Cod.

Interpretation: early street pictures can be quick, funny, and visually crowded. Cape Light is more spacious, patient, luminous, and contemplative. Warm interior light against cool outdoor light is a useful recurring relationship. Do not describe the entire career as pastel or serene.

## Limits and open questions

- Mood words are critical interpretations. They are not measurable properties or claims of the artists' intentions.
- No photographer has one fixed contrast curve, palette, camera, or film recipe across all periods.
- A scan, a book reproduction, and a photographic print can differ in contrast and colour.
- Exact film stocks, lenses, exposure settings, and printing methods require evidence for a specific work. Do not invent universal settings.
- A deeper study could compare specified prints or book editions side by side. The user has not specified a period or reproduction target.
