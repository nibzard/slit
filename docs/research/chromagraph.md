# Chromagraph Camera research

Research date: October 29, 2026.

Scope: Public sources. The app was not installed or tested.
Prices, compatibility, versions, and plans can change.

## Overview

Chromagraph Camera is a photography app from Chromatic Applications LLC.
It captures raw images and applies processing designed by cinematographer Steve Yedlin.
The developer links its color and texture rendering to the Knives Out films.
The app combines capture, editing, and a gallery.

Source: [Official product page](https://www.chromaticapplications.com/chromagraph/chromagraph).

## People and origin

- Steve Yedlin designed the image processing.
- Eric Cameron worked on the software and color science.
- Rian Johnson requested the app for his personal photography.

Johnson announced the app on October 28, 2026.
The team initially built it for personal use, then released it publicly.

Source: [Launch background](https://www.bottlerocketcontent.com/rian-johnson-steve-yedlin-chromagraph-iphone-camera-app/).

## Capture and editing

1. Frame the photograph in the viewfinder.
2. Capture the photograph.
3. Open the gallery to see the processed look.
4. Adjust the photograph.
5. Export a finished copy.

The viewfinder does not preview the finished look.
The gallery retains full-quality raw originals and applies adjustments during viewing.
The app exports JPEG or High Efficiency Image Container (HEIC) files.
Preferences includes automatic export to the photo library.

Source: [Official workflow](https://www.chromaticapplications.com/chromagraph/chromagraph).

Launch statements identify controls for color, exposure, tone, texture, grain, and halation.
Halation describes the glow around bright areas associated with film.
Yedlin describes the processing as his film mathematics adapted to Apple camera behavior.
The controls allow substantial changes within the app's overall style.
The gallery applies edits without permanently changing the originals.
The film connection does not establish equivalent results across phone and cinema cameras.

Sources: [Yedlin's explanation and samples](https://www.newsshooter.com/2026/10/28/chromagraph-camera-ios-app-by-steve-yedlin-asc/),
[Johnson's statements](https://brobible.com/culture/article/rian-johnson-iphone-camera-app-chromagraph/).

## Limits and plans

- The current app captures still photographs only.
- Johnson mentions possible future video support, likely restricted to recent Pro iPhones.
- No video release date or commitment was found.
- Launch reporting says an Android version is not planned.
- Johnson cites the work needed to tune processing across different Android devices.

Source: [Platform limits and launch statements](https://brobible.com/culture/article/rian-johnson-iphone-camera-app-chromagraph/).

## Privacy

The privacy policy was updated on October 13, 2026.
It states that camera content is processed on the device.
The company does not upload camera content or keep server copies.
It states that the app does not collect, store, or share personal information.

Location tagging is optional. Location metadata can remain in shared photographs.
Sharing through another service follows that service's privacy policy.
Camera, photo, and location permissions are managed in iOS Settings.

Apple displays "Data Not Collected" and says the developer's declaration is not verified by Apple.
These are published declarations, not findings from an independent privacy audit.

Sources: [Privacy policy](https://www.chromaticapplications.com/chromagraph/privacy),
[App Store disclosure](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/chromagraph-camera/id6803329435).

## Samples and early reviews

- [Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2026/10/28/chromagraph-camera-ios-app-by-steve-yedlin-asc/) includes interface screenshots and ten sample photographs attributed to Yedlin and friends.
- [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/26/10/28/rian-johnsons-chromagraph-camera-app-brings-his-cinematic-style-to-your-iphone-photos) includes a comparison with the standard iPhone camera. The reviewer finds capture simple, dislikes the default style, and finds editing less intuitive than Apple Photos.
- [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/rian-johnson-steve-yedlin-app) gives positive early impressions and suggests location scouting and reference photography as uses.

These are early impressions. No controlled comparison across supported phones and lighting conditions was found.
